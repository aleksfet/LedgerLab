Depreciation Calculator

Depreciation Calculator

Straight-Line Depreciation, explained step by step.

When a business buys a long-term asset — like a van, computer, or furniture — it is usually recorded as an asset first, not expensed all at once. Depreciation then shows how much of that asset's cost has been used up during each accounting period.

Under the matching principle, a business spreads an asset's cost across the years it helps generate revenue — so each year carries only its fair share.

Purpose of the Income Statement

The income statement shows whether a business earned a profit or had a loss during a specific period. It helps owners and managers understand revenue, control expenses, and evaluate operating performance.

What this statement tells you

  • The income statement shows whether the business earned more than it spent during the period.
  • Net income means revenue was greater than expenses.
  • Net loss means expenses were greater than revenue.

What this means

Depreciation lowers profit but isn't a cash payment this period — your cash didn't drop by this amount. It spreads the cost of a long-term asset over its useful life.

What each activity means

  • Operating — cash from the day-to-day running of the business, like selling to customers and paying rent and wages.
  • Investing — cash from buying or selling long-term assets, like equipment.
  • Financing — cash from owners and lenders, like owner contributions, withdrawals, and loans.

Why profit isn't the same as cash

  • Sale on account — revenue and profit can go up before the cash is actually collected.
  • Buy equipment for cash — cash drops now, but the whole purchase isn't an immediate expense on the income statement.
  • Owner invests cash — cash goes up, but it's financing, not revenue or profit.
